# Gates vs. Proxies, and Finding vs. Filtering

Two audits that run before any evidence is weighed. Both exist because the
standard pipeline loses talent *directionally* — systematically rejecting the
non-standard excellent while waving through the standard adequate — and the
losses happen upstream of anything an interview can fix.

## Part 1 — The gates audit

### Legitimate gates

Hard, binary facts that genuinely disqualify. Screening on them is not bias;
it is honesty:

- Right to work in the country
- The language fluency the work actually demands
- A mandatory licence, certification, or clearance
- Genuine, non-negotiable presence where the work truly requires it

Two rules:

1. **Stated first.** At the top of the advert, in plain language, before
   anyone invests an hour of hope in an application they were never eligible
   to make. A hard no buried at the bottom — or discovered at screening — is
   a small, avoidable cruelty. A good process front-loads its honest nos.
2. **That's the whole list.** If a "requirement" is not one of these four
   kinds of fact, it is a proxy, and it goes in the next section.

### Proxies dressed as gates

Everything else commonly written as a knockout is a proxy for something the
author found hard to articulate:

| proxy | what it is actually trying to say | what it filters out |
|-------|----------------------------------|---------------------|
| "5+ years experience" | "can operate without supervision" | the fast learner with 3 brilliant years |
| exact title match | "has done work like this" | everyone whose company named the role differently |
| named degree / credential | "can reason rigorously in this domain" | the self-taught person with the public work to prove it |
| "no employment gaps" | nothing — this one is pure noise | carers, career changers, founders whose company failed |
| keyword density | "familiar with our stack" | the person who writes prose instead of listing acronyms |
| "culture fit" (undefined) | usually: "reminds us of us" | difference — the entire point of the hire |

The audit: for each proxy in the draft requirement, write down the real thing
it stands for. That real thing becomes an evidence question ("what would
demonstrate this?") — answerable by a work sample, an artifact, a reference —
and the proxy is **demoted to a soft signal**, never a knockout. The Role
Architect's brief includes checking this demotion actually happened.

Why this is worth the effort: in the Harvard Business School / Accenture
"Hidden Workers" research, 88% of 2,250 surveyed employers admitted qualified,
high-skilled candidates were screened out for not matching the job
description's language — 94% for middle-skilled roles. Proxy knockouts do not
lose candidates at random; they lose precisely the non-linear paths and
unusual combinations that make a person capable of elevating a team.

## Part 2 — The finding plan

A funnel is subtractive — it starts with everyone and removes. Finding is
additive — it starts with no one and seeks. The genuinely excellent are
usually employed, not desperate, and not spraying applications into portals;
you do not filter your way to them. Before treating the inbound pile as the
candidate universe, record in `02-decision.md` which of these will actually
be worked, by whom, by when:

- **Evidence-of-work search.** Treat the professional networks as a search
  engine for the thing people have actually built, shipped, written, or
  spoken about — not the title they hold. Outbound, deliberate,
  one-at-a-time. It scales badly, which is why it works: it forces looking
  at people as individuals rather than rows.
- **The public record of how people think.** Open-source contributions, side
  projects, essays, talks, community answers. Demonstrated capability rather
  than claimed credential — visible, and ungameable by a keyword optimiser.
- **Where people do the work, not where they apply for it.** Hackathons,
  build weekends, meetups, small think-and-build gatherings. Two hours of
  watching someone reason through a problem teaches more than any number of
  structured interviews — judgement under real conditions, not rehearsed
  answers.
- **Engineered serendipity.** The meetup, the conference corridor, the
  coffee introduction. Not soft alternatives to real recruiting — how much
  of the best hiring has always happened, because you see the person think
  in the wild. Put deliberate volume behind it rather than leaving it to
  chance.
- **Trusted judgement, with the echo guard.** A specific, credible person
  saying "this one is exceptional, and here is precisely why" is the
  strongest single signal available. But a network is itself a filter, and a
  network that only refers people like itself rebuilds the monoculture by
  hand. Lean on referrals for *judgement*; consciously reach beyond the
  circle for *difference*.
- **The real headhunter, for the roles that warrant one.** The good ones do
  not run a funnel; they hold a map — they know which signals are real,
  can tell a proxy from a true requirement, and go find the person. Pay for
  understanding, not throughput: a fee structure that rewards speed-to-fill
  trains anyone toward the template that converts fastest, and the template
  is the average.
- **AI pointed at finding, not rejecting.** Used as a rejection tool, AI
  narrows the field to keyword matches and reproduces the past. Used as
  discovery, it can surface the non-obvious but genuinely relevant — the
  unusual background that maps onto the need in a way no human had time to
  spot. Same tool, opposite intent, entirely different result.

**The rule the memo enforces:** a shortlist that is 100% inbound-funnel is a
stated finding, and the verdict rules in SKILL.md treat it as invalidating
the comparison set — "best of who applied" is not "best of who we could
find." The finding plan runs before an offer is made.

## The dilution cascade

Why the requirement must be written by the role-owner, in their own words —
the five lossy hand-offs this skill is structured to prevent:

1. **The JD is written from a template** by someone who does not fully
   understand the role; the differentiating requirement becomes a generic
   competency list.
2. **The recruiter re-reads it** through every similar role they have placed;
   it drifts toward the average of all roles like it.
3. **The tools regress it to the mean** — JD generators and keyword
   optimisers are trained on a corpus of generic descriptions and pull
   everything toward its centre.
4. **The filter screens for the legible, not the good** — and inverts the
   signal, punishing exactly the paths and combinations that carry value.
5. **The interviewer hires for resemblance** — affinity dressed up as
   culture fit; even the survivors are selected for sameness.

The single most powerful fix is to compress the chain: keep the person who
actually understands the role close to the search — writing the real
requirement, reading the real evidence. And one honest caution that comes
with it: the role-owner in the loop only helps if they bring what the machine
cannot. Opening a chat window and accepting a fluent, confident,
beautifully-formatted draft JD as if the thinking were done is the same
cascade with fewer steps. AI amplifies whatever understanding it is pointed
through: name the specific, hard-to-articulate thing the role needs *first*,
then let the tool sharpen it. Presence is not the same as judgement.
