# Decision statement

Fill this from the role-owner's own words. Do not infer the missing parts —
a gap here is the first finding, and every role will silently resolve an
ambiguity in a different direction.

## Which decision is this?
<open a role? / sourcing plan? / choose between finalists? / hire this
specific person? A run covers at most two adjacent ones.>

## The irreducible requirement
<The specific, differentiating thing this role must do that the current team
cannot — one sentence, in the role-owner's words, not a template's. If it
reads like it could head any similar advert, it is not yet the requirement.>

## The alternatives, explicitly
<Not hiring. Contracting. Automating. Reshaping an existing role. Splitting
the role. "Whether to hire X" is really "X vs. the best person a real search
would find vs. not hiring at all".>

## Date and forcing function
<By when, and what actually forces that date.>

## Desired outcome
<Observable, at six months. "Team is less stretched" is not an outcome.
"Ingestion pages drop below 2/week and the two data epics ship" is.>

## The sourcing plan (finding, not just filtering)
<Which finding channels will actually be worked, by whom, by when — see
references/gates-and-finding.md. If the honest answer is "post and wait",

## Success criteria (6 months out)
<What must be observably true for this to have been the right call. Written
before the analysis, for the same reason trial thresholds are written before
the trial.>

## Reversibility
<Probation reality, notice periods, what unwinding actually costs — a senior
hire is far more of a one-way door than the org chart implies, and the
process should be scaled accordingly.>
